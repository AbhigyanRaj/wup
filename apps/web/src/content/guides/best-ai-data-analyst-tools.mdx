The best AI data analyst tool is the one that connects to where your data already lives and shows its work. As of October 2026, general assistants (ChatGPT, Claude) are strongest on uploaded files, spreadsheet copilots (Gemini in Sheets, Copilot in Excel) on the file you have open, notebook and BI platforms (Hex, ThoughtSpot) on data warehouses, Julius on files and Google Sheets, and wuup on MongoDB, Google Sheets and PDFs. Disclosure up front: **we build wuup**. Below are the criteria we used, then each tool with what it connects to, how it prices and its limits.

## How we compared them

A tool that writes beautiful prose about your data is not useful if the numbers are wrong. These six criteria matter more than which model a tool uses:

1. **Data sources.** Does it connect to your database, warehouse or sheet, or only accept uploads? Live connections avoid stale snapshots.
2. **Grounding.** Is every number computed from data by a query or code, or written by the model? See [why AI data answers hallucinate](/guides/why-ai-data-answers-hallucinate) for what goes wrong without it.
3. **Transparency.** Can you see the query, range or code behind each answer, and check it?
4. **Safety.** Is database access read-only? Are sensitive fields hidden from the model? Our [guide to connecting a database to AI safely](/guides/is-it-safe-to-connect-database-to-ai) lists twelve controls to look for.
5. **Pricing shape.** Per seat, per credit, per message, or bring your own model key. The shape decides what heavy use costs.
6. **Fit.** Who it is for: an analyst in a notebook, a manager in a sheet, a developer with a production database.

We did not run a scored benchmark, and nothing below is an objective ranking. Each tool's facts were checked against the vendor's own pages in October 2026; plans and prices change often, so confirm before you buy.

## General assistants: ChatGPT and Claude

**ChatGPT.** Upload a CSV or Excel file and ChatGPT writes and runs code against it, so sums and group-bys are computed rather than guessed. OpenAI's [file uploads FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8555545-file-uploads-faq) puts spreadsheets at roughly 50 MB depending on row size, with a 512 MB cap per file and lower upload limits on the free plan. Paid plans add connectors such as Google Drive. *Limits:* uploads are snapshots, so you upload again whenever the data changes. *Checked October 2026.*

**Claude.** Anthropic's [upload guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-what-kinds-of-documents-can-i-upload-to-claude) allows files up to 500 MB and 20 files per chat; CSV works directly and XLSX needs code execution and file creation enabled. Claude is strong at turning an analysis into a written explanation. *Limits:* the same snapshot problem as any upload; very large tables can still exceed what fits in one conversation. *Checked October 2026.*

**Choose these when** you have an export and one deep question, and a copy of the data in a chat account is acceptable. For repeat questions on changing data, a connected tool fits better. ## Spreadsheet copilots: Gemini in Sheets and Copilot in Excel

**Gemini in Google Sheets.** The side panel can generate analysis and insights, build charts, create pivot tables and apply formatting, and it reads the open spreadsheet plus Drive and Gmail when asked, per [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/14356410?hl=en). The `=AI()` function fills cells from a prompt and a range, with a per-batch cap listed on its [help page](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en). Requires an eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan. *Limits:* one file at a time; generated charts do not update when data changes. *Checked October 2026.*

**Copilot in Excel.** Microsoft's [FAQ](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/copilot/frequently-asked-questions-about-copilot-in-excel) lists the licenses that include it: Microsoft 365 Personal or Family with an AI credits plan, Microsoft 365 Premium, a commercial Microsoft 365 Copilot subscription, or an eligible business or enterprise subscription with Copilot Chat. It edits the workbook directly. *Limits:* works inside the workbook; some file states (such as checked-out SharePoint files on desktop) are not supported. *Checked October 2026.*

**Choose these when** your data already lives in the spreadsheet and your organisation pays for the suite. They are the lowest-friction option and keep data inside your existing vendor.

## Analyst tools: Julius, Hex and ThoughtSpot

**Julius.** A chat-based analyst for uploaded files that also connects to Google Sheets and Google Drive and runs code to produce charts and tables. Pricing is a free tier with a monthly message allowance and paid monthly plans; database connections are on higher plans. See [julius.ai/pricing](https://julius.ai/pricing) for current tiers, which have changed several times. *Limits:* message-based allowances can run out on heavy use. *Checked October 2026; the pricing page details vary by region and billing period.*

**Hex.** A collaborative notebook (SQL, Python, charts) with an AI agent, aimed at data teams. Its [pricing page](https://hex.tech/pricing/) lists a free Community plan that can connect any data source, Professional at 36 dollars per editor per month and Team at 75 dollars per editor per month, with Enterprise by quote; the Hex agent is included on Professional and Team and uses monthly credits. *Limits:* built for people comfortable in notebooks; non-technical users typically consume published apps rather than ask questions directly. *Checked October 2026.*

**ThoughtSpot.** Search and AI analytics on top of a cloud data warehouse, with an AI analyst called Spotter. Its [pricing page](https://www.thoughtspot.com/pricing) lists Essentials from 25 dollars per user per month billed annually, Pro from 0.10 dollars per credit, and custom Enterprise pricing, with live connections to warehouses such as Snowflake, Databricks and Redshift. *Limits:* it needs a modelled warehouse to shine; it is a BI platform rollout, not a quick connect. *Checked October 2026.*

**Choose these when** your data is in a warehouse and you have, or are, a data team. They offer far more modelling and governance than a chat tool, at more setup cost.

## How to choose

- **Your data is a file you exported.** ChatGPT or Claude.
- **Your data is the spreadsheet you live in.** Gemini in Sheets or Copilot in Excel, matching your suite.
- **You need per-row AI formulas.** `=AI()` in Sheets or an add-on, not a chat tool.
- **Your data is in a warehouse and you have analysts.** Hex for notebooks, ThoughtSpot for company-wide search.
- **Your data is in MongoDB or Google Sheets and you want plain-English answers with the query shown.** wuup.

Whatever you pick, run the same three test questions on your own data: one you already know the answer to, one with an ambiguous term, and one that needs a total across all rows. The tool that shows its query and gets all three right is the one to keep.

## FAQ

### Which AI data analyst tool is free?

Most have a free tier with limits. ChatGPT, Claude and Hex have free plans; Julius has a free tier with a message allowance; wuup gives 50 free questions and then works with your own model key at no charge from wuup.

### Can these tools write to my database?

Spreadsheet copilots edit the open workbook by design. For databases, prefer tools that connect read-only. wuup's MongoDB connection is read-only, and it rejects write stages such as `$out` and `$merge` even if your database user has write rights.

### Is it safe to upload company data to ChatGPT or Claude?

It depends on your plan, your company policy and the provider's data terms. Business and enterprise plans generally offer stronger data controls than consumer plans. Check with whoever owns data policy before uploading customer data.

### Do I need SQL or MongoDB skills?

Not to get answers, but basic reading skills help you verify them. Tools that show the query let you check filters and date ranges even if you could not write the query yourself.
